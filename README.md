# HMTD

This repository documents the historical discovery of HMTD , with the original sources provided as PDFs. Some of the articles were originally written in German. English translations are also included.

## Brief History

HMTD was first synthesized in 1855 by the German chemist L. Legler. His article concerned the oxidation of diethyl ether, but he serendipitously obtained "Lamp acid" by oxidizing diethyl ether with O₂ over Pt under harsh conditions. He subsequently treated Lamp acid with ammonia to afford HMTD. Notably, Legler was unable to correctly identify the structure of Lamp acid, and his synthetic method was inefficient; he reported obtaining only a trace amount of HT.

In 1897, J. U. Nef identified the Lamp acid obtained by Legler as a trimer of BHMP. He also elucidated the mechanism of Legler's oxidation: diethyl ether is oxidized to give formaldehyde, hydrogen peroxide, acetaldehyde, acetic acid, and formic acid, among which hydrogen peroxide and formaldehyde react to form BHMP (Lamp acid).

In 1900, Baeyer and Villiger, building on Nef's work, provided an accurate account of how Legler had obtained HMTD through the oxidation of diethyl ether. They also determined the chemical structure of HMTD, which remains the accepted structure today. Furthermore, they modified the synthetic procedure by employing ammonium sulfate, formaldehyde, and hydrogen peroxide. However, they did not report the yield of HT obtained by this method.

In 1912, Girsewald reported an efficient synthesis of HMTD based on the decomposition of hexamine with hydrogen peroxide in the presence of citric acid. This method is still widely used today and remains the most efficient route known. He reported a yield of 64%. Through explosion experiments, he also demonstrated that HMTD is more hazardous than mercury fulminate.
